\begin{abstract}
We study divisibility questions for integers obtained by concatenation in positional notation.
For fixed digit length, concatenating an arithmetic progression is an affine function of its
initial term, which yields an exact congruence criterion.  We use this to prove a global
existence theorem in every even base; in decimal this resolves the existence conjectures
recorded in OEIS A075001 and A075000.

We then consider the Champernowne prefixes
\[
P(k)=1\concat2\concat\cdots\concat k.
\]
Our main theorem proves that for every positive integer \(n\) there exists \(k\) such that
\(n\mid P(k)\), thereby resolving the well-definedness conjecture in OEIS A075002.  The
proof has two local ingredients.  For primes \(p\notin\{2,3,5\}\), fixed-digit blocks
admit unit translations and explicit state periods.  The base primes \(2\) and \(5\) are
handled by the terminal block, while \(3\) requires a separate block-shift lifting argument.
The local roots are synchronized at one common prefix index by processing odd prime factors
in increasing order; every completed state period has no prime divisor larger than the prime
just processed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let
\[
P(k)=1\concat2\concat\cdots\concat k
\]
denote the decimal integer obtained by concatenating \(1,2,\ldots,k\), and for \(n\ge1\) let
\[
C_n(k)=k\concat(k+1)\concat\cdots\concat(k+n-1).
\]
OEIS A075001 asks for the smallest \(k\) such that \(n\mid C_n(k)\), with value \(0\)
if no such \(k\) exists, while OEIS A075000 records the corresponding smallest quotient
\cite{oeis75001,oeis75000}.  OEIS A075002 asks for the smallest \(k\) such that
\(n\mid P(k)\), where \(P\) is also the sequence in OEIS A007908
\cite{oeis75002,oeis77908}.  On September 11, 2026, the three entries still recorded
these existence statements as conjectures.  We are not aware of a previous proof of the
full A075002 assertion.

Our first result is a general even-base construction for concatenated arithmetic progressions.
The second and main result is the complete existence theorem for Champernowne prefixes.
The proof strategy is modular.  We first choose a single sufficiently long digit block.  The
endpoint is made divisible by the factor of the modulus whose prime divisors divide the base, and the exceptional
\(3\)-power is installed by a three-way block shift.  The remaining odd prime powers are then
installed in increasing order, using state periods to preserve all earlier congruences.  The
fact that a completed state period has no prime divisor larger than the prime already processed
ensures that the next translation remains a unit; a finite capacity bound keeps every chosen
endpoint in the same digit block.

Concatenation of arithmetic progressions in arbitrary bases has also been studied by Luca and
Teguia Tabuguia, who derive explicit formulae and prove non-holonomicity
\cite{luca-teguia}.  Our emphasis is different: divisibility by an externally prescribed
modulus and, in particular, the three OEIS existence problems above.

\section{Fixed-digit arithmetic progressions}
\label{sec:fixed-digit-ap}

Let \(\beta\ge2\), let \(n\ge1\), let \(s\in\mathbb Z\), and let \(d\ge1\).  Put
\(B=\beta^d\).  Suppose that
\[
k,\ k+s,\ \ldots,\ k+(n-1)s
\]
are positive \(d\)-digit base-\(\beta\) integers.  Their concatenation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ap-concat}
C_{\beta,n,s,d}(k)
  =\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(k+js)B^{\,n-1-j}.
\end{equation}
Define
\[
R_n(B)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}B^i,
\qquad
T_n(B)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}jB^{\,n-1-j}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ap-affine}
C_{\beta,n,s,d}(k)=kR_n(B)+sT_n(B).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-digit affine congruence criterion]\label{prop:fixed-digit-criterion}
Under the fixed-digit hypotheses above, let \(g=\gcd(R_n(B),n)\).  The
congruence in the affine expression \eqref{eq:ap-affine}, viewed as a congruence
in \(k\in\mathbb Z\),
\[
n\mid C_{\beta,n,s,d}(k)
\]
is solvable in \(k\) if and only if
\[
g\mid sT_n(B).
\]
When solvable, the solutions form one residue class modulo \(n/g\).
This congruence criterion concerns solutions in \(\mathbb Z\); admissibility of a
representative in a particular finite fixed-digit interval is a separate condition.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:ap-affine}, the desired divisibility is the linear congruence
\[
kR_n(B)\equiv-sT_n(B)\pmod n.
\]
The standard solvability criterion for a linear congruence gives the claim.  After division
by \(g\), the coefficient \(R_n(B)/g\) is invertible modulo \(n/g\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Even-base AP divisibility]\label{thm:even-base-ap}
Let \(\beta\ge2\) be even.  For every \(n\ge1\) and every \(s\in\mathbb Z\), there
exist \(d\ge1\) and \(k\) such that all
\[
k,\ k+s,\ldots,k+(n-1)s
\]
are positive \(d\)-digit base-\(\beta\) integers and
\[
n\mid C_{\beta,n,s,d}(k).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ap-factorization}
n=qm,
\end{equation}
where \(q\) is the largest divisor of \(n\) all of whose prime divisors divide \(\beta\).  Thus
\((q,m)=1\) and \((m,\beta)=1\).

Choose \(d\) arbitrarily large subject to
\[
q\mid\beta^d,
\qquad
\beta^d\equiv1\pmod m.
\]
Such \(d\) exist: the first condition holds for all sufficiently large \(d\), and the
second holds whenever \(d\) is a multiple of \(\ord_m(\beta)\), with no order condition
when \(m=1\).

Put \(B=\beta^d\), and let
\[
u_- =\min\{0,(n-1)s\},
\qquad
u_+ =\max\{0,(n-1)s\}.
\]
All blocks have \(d\) digits whenever
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ap-window}
\beta^{d-1}-u_-
\le k\le
\beta^d-1-u_+.
\end{equation}
The length of this interval tends to infinity with \(d\), so we may choose \(k\) in it such
that
\[
k+(n-1)s\equiv0\pmod q.
\]
Modulo \(q\), every term of the sum in \eqref{eq:ap-concat} except the final block is
multiplied by a positive power of \(B\).  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ap-smooth}
C_{\beta,n,s,d}(k)\equiv k+(n-1)s\equiv0\pmod q.
\end{equation}
Modulo \(m\), since \(B\equiv1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ap-coprime}
C_{\beta,n,s,d}(k)
 \equiv nk+s\frac{n(n-1)}2\pmod m.
\end{equation}
The first term is divisible by \(m\).  If \(n\) is odd, then
\[
s\frac{n(n-1)}2=mqs\frac{n-1}{2}.
\]
If \(n\) is even, then \(2\mid\beta\), so the full \(2\)-part of \(n\) lies in \(q\);
hence \(q/2\in\mathbb Z\), and
\[
s\frac{n(n-1)}2=m\frac q2\,s(n-1).
\]
Thus the concatenation is \(0\) modulo both coprime factors \(q\) and \(m\), hence modulo
\(n\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A075001]\label{cor:A075001}
For every \(n\ge1\) there exists a positive integer \(k\) such that
\[
n\mid k\concat(k+1)\concat\cdots\concat(k+n-1).
\]
Hence OEIS A075001 has no zero terms.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Take \(\beta=10\) and \(s=1\) in Theorem~\ref{thm:even-base-ap}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A075000]\label{cor:A075000}
For every \(n\ge1\), OEIS A075000 is nonzero.  More precisely,
\[
A075000(n)=\frac{C_n(A075001(n))}{n}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Existence follows from Corollary~\ref{cor:A075001}.  It remains only to justify minimality.
For fixed \(n\), if \(k_2>k_1\), every term in the tuple for \(k_2\) is larger than the
corresponding term for \(k_1\), so every corresponding digit length is at least as large.
If the total concatenated lengths differ, \(C_n(k_2)\) is longer and hence numerically
larger; if they agree, the coordinatewise length inequalities force equality of every
corresponding block length, and the first position where the concatenated strings differ
lies in an equal-length block whose decimal representation is larger for \(k_2\).
Thus \(C_n(k_2)>C_n(k_1)\), including across powers of ten.  The least divisible
concatenation therefore occurs at the least witnessing index \(A075001(n)\), and division
by the fixed positive integer \(n\) preserves the ordering.
\end{proof}

\section{Fixed-block identities for Champernowne prefixes}
\label{sec:fixed-block-identities}

Fix a digit length \(d\) and put \(B=10^d\).  Let \(k\ge1\) and \(L\ge0\), and suppose
that every integer in the appended interval \(k+1,\ldots,k+L\) remains in this same
\(d\)-digit block (with the condition vacuous when \(L=0\)).  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-transition}
P(k+L)
 =B^LP(k)+\sum_{j=1}^L(k+j)B^{L-j}.
\end{equation}
Set
\[
S_L=\sum_{r=0}^{L-1}B^r,
\qquad
T_L=\sum_{j=1}^LjB^{L-j}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shift-identity}
P(k+L)-P(k)
 =(B^L-1)P(k)+kS_L+T_L.
\end{equation}
A direct telescoping calculation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geometric-T}
(B-1)T_L=BS_L-L.
\end{equation}

\section{Local lifting and state periods}
\label{sec:local-lifting}

\begin{lemma}[Unit translation]\label{lem:unit-translation}
Fix a \(d\)-digit block and put \(B=10^d\).  Let \(p\) be an odd prime with
\(p\nmid B(B-1)\), and let
\[
h=\ord_p(B),
\qquad
U=T_h,
\]
let \(a\ge0\), and let \(c\ge1\) with \(p\nmid c\).  Put \(L=chp^a\).  If
\(k\ge1\) and every integer \(k+1,\ldots,k+L\) remains in the same fixed
\(d\)-digit block, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unit-translation}
P(k+L)-P(k)\equiv cp^aU\pmod{p^{a+1}},
\end{equation}
Moreover \(p\nmid U\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Modulo \(p\), \(B^h=1\) and \(S_h=0\).  From \eqref{eq:geometric-T},
\[
(B-1)U\equiv-h\pmod p.
\]
Since \(h\mid p-1\), both \(h\) and \(B-1\) are units modulo \(p\), hence so is \(U\).

By the lifting-the-exponent lemma,
\[
B^{chp^a}\equiv1\pmod{p^{a+1}}.
\]
Since \(B-1\) is a unit, this also gives
\[
S_L\equiv0\pmod{p^{a+1}}.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:geometric-T} therefore yields
\[
T_L\equiv-\frac{chp^a}{B-1}\equiv cp^aU
\pmod{p^{a+1}}.
\]
Substitution into \eqref{eq:shift-identity} proves \eqref{eq:unit-translation}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-block state period]\label{lem:state-period}
Fix a \(d\)-digit block and put \(B=10^d\).  Let \(p\) be an odd prime with
\(p\nmid B(B-1)\), and let \(e\ge1\).  With
\begin{equation}\label{eq:state-period-length}
H=p^e\ord_{p^e}(B)
\end{equation}
and \(k\ge1\), if every integer \(k+1,\ldots,k+H\) remains in the same fixed
\(d\)-digit block, then \(H\) is a period of the endpoint state modulo \(p^e\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:state-period}
P(k+H)\equiv P(k)\pmod{p^e},
\qquad
k+H\equiv k\pmod{p^e}.
\end{equation}
More generally, for every \(m\ge1\), the same two congruences with \(H\) replaced
by \(mH\) hold provided every integer \(k+1,\ldots,k+mH\) remains in that same
fixed \(d\)-digit block.  Every prime divisor of \(H\) is at most \(p\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(r=\ord_{p^e}(B)\).  Since \(r\mid H\), \(B^H\equiv1\pmod{p^e}\).  Because
\(B-1\) is a unit, \(S_H\equiv0\pmod{p^e}\).  Also \(p^e\mid H\), so
\eqref{eq:geometric-T} gives \(T_H\equiv0\pmod{p^e}\).  Equation
\eqref{eq:shift-identity} proves the first congruence in
\eqref{eq:state-period}, and \(p^e\mid H\) proves the second.  If \(m\ge1\) and
the longer appended interval remains in the block, the same calculation applies with
\(mH\) in place of \(H\): \(r\mid mH\), \(p^e\mid mH\), and hence
\(P(k+mH)\equiv P(k)\pmod{p^e}\) and \(k+mH\equiv k\pmod{p^e}\).

Finally,
\[
\ord_{p^e}(B)\mid\varphi(p^e)=p^{e-1}(p-1),
\]
so every prime divisor of \(H\) is at most \(p\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exceptional \(3\)-adic block shift]\label{lem:3-adic-shift}
Fix \(d\ge1\), put \(B=10^d\), and let \(L\ge1\) satisfy \(v_3(L)=a\ge1\).
If \(k\ge1\) and every integer \(k+1,\ldots,k+L\) remains in the same fixed
\(d\)-digit block, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3-adic-block-shift}
P(k+L)-P(k)
\equiv Lk+\frac{L(L+1)}2
\pmod{3^{a+1}}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(B=1+c\).  Since \(B=10^d\), we have \(9\mid c\).  Expansion of the geometric
sum gives
\[
S_L-L=\sum_{s\ge1}c^s\binom{L}{s+1}.
\]
For \(m=s+1\),
\[
\binom{L}{m}=\frac{L}{m}\binom{L-1}{m-1},
\]
so
\[
v_3\binom{L}{m}\ge a-v_3(m)
\]
and \(v_3(c)\ge2\).  For \(s\ge1\), \(v_3(s+1)\le s\), so every nonzero
correction term on the right has valuation at least
\(2s+a-v_3(s+1)\ge a+1\).  Hence
\[
S_L\equiv L\pmod{3^{a+1}}.
\]

Similarly,
\[
T_L-\frac{L(L+1)}2
 =\sum_{s\ge1}c^s\binom{L+1}{s+2}.
\]
From
\[
\binom{L+1}{m}
 =\frac{L(L+1)}{m(m-1)}\binom{L-1}{m-2}
\]
we obtain
\[
v_3\!\left(c^s\binom{L+1}{s+2}\right)
 \ge 2s+a-v_3((s+1)(s+2)).
\]
For \(s\ge1\),
\[
v_3((s+1)(s+2))\le 2s-1;
\]
for \(s=1\) this is immediate, and for \(s\ge2\) it follows from
\(3^{2s}>(s+1)(s+2)\).  Thus every correction term is divisible by \(3^{a+1}\).

Finally LTE gives \(B^L\equiv1\pmod{3^{a+2}}\), so the first term in
\eqref{eq:shift-identity} vanishes modulo \(3^{a+1}\).  Substituting the two congruences for
\(S_L\) and \(T_L\) into \eqref{eq:shift-identity} proves \eqref{eq:3-adic-block-shift}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[\(3\)-adic lifting]\label{cor:3-adic-lifting}
Fix a \(d\)-digit block with \(B=10^d\).  Let \(R=2^\alpha5^\beta\), let \(k\ge1\),
suppose \(R\mid k\) and \(3^a\mid P(k)\) with \(a\ge1\), and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3-adic-step}
L=\operatorname{lcm}(2\cdot3^a,R)=2c3^a,
\qquad 3\nmid c.
\end{equation}
Assume that every integer \(k+1,\ldots,k+2L\) remains in the same fixed \(d\)-digit
block.  Then exactly one of
\[
k,\qquad k+L,\qquad k+2L
\]
is a root modulo \(3^{a+1}\), while all three endpoint indices remain divisible by \(R\).
Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3-adic-period}
2\cdot3^e
\end{equation}
is a state period modulo \(3^e\) for every \(e\ge1\), provided the relevant appended
interval remains in the same fixed digit block.  More generally, every positive multiple
of \(2\cdot3^e\) is such a state period modulo \(3^e\) when its entire appended interval
remains in that block.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For \(t=0\), the desired quotient is \(0\).  For \(t=1,2\), we have
\(v_3(tL)=a\), and the preceding lemma applies to the full interval
\(k+1,\ldots,k+2L\).  It gives
\[
P(k+tL)-P(k)
 \equiv tLk+\frac{tL(tL+1)}2\pmod{3^{a+1}}.
\]
The right-hand side is divisible by \(3^a\), so division by \(3^a\), followed by
reduction modulo \(3\), gives for all \(t=0,1,2\)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:3-adic-sweep}
\frac{P(k+tL)-P(k)}{3^a}
 \equiv tc(2k+1)\pmod3.
\end{equation}
Since \(3\mid P(k)\) and \(10\equiv1\pmod3\),
\[
0\equiv P(k)\equiv1+2+\cdots+k=\frac{k(k+1)}2\pmod3,
\]
so \(k\equiv0\) or \(2\pmod3\).  Hence \(c(2k+1)\) is a unit modulo \(3\).  Therefore
\[
\frac{P(k+tL)}{3^a}
 \equiv \frac{P(k)}{3^a}+tc(2k+1)\pmod3,
\]
and \(t=0,1,2\) gives all three residue classes modulo \(3\), so exactly one choice
is a root modulo \(3^{a+1}\).  Because \(R\mid L\), all three endpoint indices remain
divisible by \(R\).

For the period assertion, let \(m\ge1\) and take \(L=m(2\cdot3^e)\) in
Lemma~\ref{lem:3-adic-shift}.  Its valuation is at least \(e\), so the resulting
congruence implies \(P(k+L)\equiv P(k)\pmod{3^e}\), while \(k+L\equiv k\pmod{3^e}\).
This application is valid only when the entire appended interval through \(k+L\) stays
inside the fixed block.
\end{proof}

\section{A common prefix for every modulus}
\label{sec:common-prefix}

\begin{theorem}[A075002]\label{thm:A075002}
For every positive integer \(n\), there exists \(k\ge1\) such that
\[
n\mid P(k).
\]
Consequently OEIS A075002 is well-defined for every \(n\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(n=1\), take \(k=1\).  Hence assume \(n>1\).
Factor
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A075002-factorization}
n=R\,3^e\prod_{i=1}^r p_i^{e_i},
\qquad
R=2^\alpha5^\beta,
\qquad
7\le p_1<\cdots<p_r.
\end{equation}
Let
\[
D=\operatorname{lcm}_{1\le i\le r}\ord_{p_i^{e_i}}(10),
\]
with \(D=1\) if \(r=0\).  We shall work in one sufficiently long \(d\)-digit block, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A075002-digit-choice}
d\equiv1\pmod D,
\qquad d\ge\max(\alpha,\beta).
\end{equation}
Such \(d\) are arbitrarily large.  Put \(B=10^d\).  Then for every \(i\),
\[
B\equiv10\pmod{p_i^{e_i}}.
\]

For the state periods and the step lengths below, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A075002-H0}
H_0=
\begin{cases}
2\cdot3^e,&e>0,\\
1,&e=0.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
For \(1\le i\le r\), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A075002-Hi}
H_i=\operatorname{lcm}\!\left(
H_{i-1},\ p_i^{e_i}\ord_{p_i^{e_i}}(10)
\right).
\end{equation}
For \(1\le i\le r\), put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A075002-Jia}
\begin{aligned}
J_{i,0}&=\operatorname{lcm}\!\left(\ord_{p_i}(10),H_{i-1},R\right),\\
J_{i,a}&=\operatorname{lcm}\!\left(\ord_{p_i}(10)p_i^a,H_{i-1},R\right)
\quad(1\le a<e_i).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Empty sums below are understood to be zero.  An explicit bound for the total forward
displacement of all candidate endpoints is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A075002-W}
W(n)=
2\sum_{a=1}^{e-1}\operatorname{lcm}(2\cdot3^a,R)
+
\sum_{i=1}^r(p_i-1)\sum_{a=0}^{e_i-1}J_{i,a}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, a \(3\)-adic stage uses one of \(tL_a\), \(0\le t\le2\), and hence contributes
at most \(2L_a\).  At a \(p_i\)-adic stage, one of \(tJ_{i,a}\), \(0\le t<p_i\), is
used, and hence contributes at most \((p_i-1)J_{i,a}\).  Thus \(W(n)\) bounds not only
the selected trajectory but also every candidate endpoint examined at every stage.
All quantities in \eqref{eq:A075002-H0}--\eqref{eq:A075002-W} depend only on \(n\).
Indeed, \(d\equiv1\pmod D\) implies
\[
10^d\equiv10\pmod{p_i^{e_i}},
\]
so the orders of \(B=10^d\) required by the state-period lemma are exactly the
corresponding orders of \(10\).

Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A075002-initial-modulus}
M_0=R\cdot3^{\min(e,1)}.
\end{equation}
Choose \(d\) in the congruence class in \eqref{eq:A075002-digit-choice} so large that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A075002-capacity}
9\cdot10^{d-1}>M_0+W(n).
\end{equation}
Let \(k_0\) be the least multiple of \(M_0\) that is at least \(10^{d-1}\).  Then
\(0\le k_0-10^{d-1}<M_0\).  Every candidate endpoint in the construction is at most
\(k_0+W(n)\), and therefore
\[
k_0+W(n)
 <10^{d-1}+M_0+W(n)
 <10^{d-1}+9\cdot10^{d-1}
 =10^d.
\]
All candidate endpoints are also at least \(k_0\ge10^{d-1}\).  Since every appended
interval lies between its current endpoint and one of these candidate endpoints, this
single capacity estimate supplies the fixed-block hypothesis for every translation,
state-period, and \(3\)-adic block-shift invocation below.

Since \(R\mid k_0\) and \(B\equiv0\pmod R\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A075002-initial-R}
P(k_0)=BP(k_0-1)+k_0\equiv0\pmod R.
\end{equation}
If \(e>0\), then \(3\mid k_0\), and because \(10\equiv1\pmod3\),
\[
P(k_0)\equiv\frac{k_0(k_0+1)}2\equiv0\pmod3.
\]

If \(e>1\), repeatedly apply Corollary~\ref{cor:3-adic-lifting}.  At stage \(a\), use
\[
L_a=\operatorname{lcm}(2\cdot3^a,R).
\]
The capacity estimate above puts the full interval through \(k+2L_a\) in the fixed block,
so the corollary applies to all three candidates.  One of the three choices raises a root
modulo \(3^a\) to a root modulo \(3^{a+1}\).  Since \(R\mid L_a\) and \(B\equiv0\pmod R\),
the transition identity gives \(P(k+tL_a)\equiv k+tL_a\equiv0\pmod R\) for every
\(t>0\), while \(t=0\) is the current root.  Thus the \(R\)-condition is preserved and
we obtain an endpoint \(k\) with
\[
R3^e\mid P(k).
\]
If \(e>0\), Corollary~\ref{cor:3-adic-lifting} supplies the conditional fixed-block state
period \(H_0=2\cdot3^e\) modulo \(3^e\); if \(e=0\), there is no \(3\)-adic condition
to preserve.

Now process \(p_1,\ldots,p_r\) in increasing order.  Suppose all preceding prime powers have
been installed at the current endpoint \(k\), and \(H_{i-1}\) is a common conditional
fixed-block state period for them.  Let \(p=p_i\) and
\[
h=\ord_p(10).
\]
Since \(B\equiv10\pmod p\), we also have \(h=\ord_p(B)\), and
\(p\nmid B(B-1)\).

Every prime factor of the period contributed by an earlier prime \(q<p\) is at most \(q\),
while \(R\) has no prime divisors other than \(2\) and \(5\).  Therefore
\(p\nmid H_{i-1}R\), and also \(p\nmid h\) since \(h\mid p-1\).  Hence
\[
J_{i,0}=c_0h,\qquad p\nmid c_0.
\]
Let \(U=T_h\).  For \(t=0\) the following congruence is trivial; for
\(1\le t<p\), apply Lemma~\ref{lem:unit-translation} with \(c=tc_0\) and
\(L=tJ_{i,0}\).  The complete interval through the largest candidate is in the fixed
block by the capacity estimate, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A075002-first-sweep}
P(k+tJ_{i,0})-P(k)\equiv tc_0U\pmod p,
\qquad 0\le t<p.
\end{equation}
Since \(p\nmid U\), the map
\[
P(k)+tc_0U,\qquad t\in\mathbb F_p,
\]
runs through all residues modulo \(p\): it is an affine permutation of \(\mathbb F_p\).
Because \(J_{i,0}\) is a multiple of \(H_{i-1}\), the fixed-block state-period lemma
preserves every previously installed prime-power condition for every candidate \(tJ_{i,0}\).
Also \(R\mid J_{i,0}\), and \(B\equiv0\pmod R\); whenever \(R\mid P(k)\), the transition
identity gives \(P(k+tJ_{i,0})\equiv k+tJ_{i,0}\equiv0\pmod R\) for \(t>0\), while
\(t=0\) is the current root.  Choose the unique \(t\) giving a root modulo \(p\).

Suppose inductively that a root modulo \(p^a\) has been obtained, where \(1\le a<e_i\).
Again \(p\nmid H_{i-1}Rh\), so \(J_{i,a}=c_ahp^a\) with \(p\nmid c_a\).  For
\(1\le t<p\), Lemma~\ref{lem:unit-translation} applied to \(tJ_{i,a}\), with the
fixed-block hypothesis supplied by the capacity estimate, gives
\[
P(k+tJ_{i,a})-P(k)\equiv tc_ap^aU\pmod{p^{a+1}}.
\]
The congruence is also trivial for \(t=0\), so it holds for \(0\le t<p\).  Since
\(P(k)\equiv0\pmod{p^a}\), division by \(p^a\) gives
\[
\frac{P(k+tJ_{i,a})}{p^a}
\equiv\frac{P(k)}{p^a}+tc_aU\pmod p,
\qquad 0\le t<p.
\]
The coefficient \(c_aU\) is a unit modulo \(p\), so the \(p\) candidate endpoints
traverse all possible next \(p\)-adic digits and exactly one is a root modulo
\(p^{a+1}\).  Because \(J_{i,a}\) is a multiple of \(H_{i-1}\) and \(R\), the
same fixed-block state-period and \(R\)-preservation arguments keep all earlier
conditions intact.  Repeating installs \(p^{e_i}\).

After completing this prime power, Lemma~\ref{lem:state-period} gives the conditional
fixed-block state period
\[
p_i^{e_i}\ord_{p_i^{e_i}}(B)
 =p_i^{e_i}\ord_{p_i^{e_i}}(10).
\]
Consequently \(H_i\) in \eqref{eq:A075002-Hi} is a common conditional state period for
all installed prime powers.  Every prime divisor of the new factor is at most \(p_i\), so
the same argument applies at the next, larger prime.

At termination, the same endpoint \(k\) satisfies every prime-power condition in the
factorization \eqref{eq:A075002-factorization}.  Therefore \(n\mid P(k)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:increasing-primes}
The increasing-prime order is essential to this proof.  A single global affine orbit need not
have unit translation simultaneously at all prime factors.  The construction above avoids that
obstruction by preserving completed states with periods whose prime divisors are all at most the
prime just processed; a larger, unprocessed prime therefore cannot divide the next step's
coefficient.
\end{remark}
